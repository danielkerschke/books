[^1] Da das Werk <g>Burthogges</g> so gut wie unbekannt geblieben ist — erst <g>Lyon</g> hat in seiner Schrift über den Idealismus in England darauf verwiesen — füge ich die entscheidenden Belege hier vollständig im Original hinzu: „As the Eye has no Perceivance of things but under <i>Colours</i>, that are not in them (and the same, with due alteration, must be said of the other Senses), so the <g>Understanding</g> apprehends not things, or any Habitudes or Aspects of them, but under <i>Certain Notions</i>, that neither have that being in Objects, or that being of Objects, that they seem to have; but are, in all respects, the very same to the mind or Understanding, that Colours are to the Eye, and Sound to the Ear. To be more particular, the Understanding conceives not any thing but under the Notion of an <i>Entity</i>, and this either a <i>Substance</i> or an <i>Accident</i>; Under that of a <i>whole</i> or of a <i>part;</i> or of a <i>Cause</i>, or of an <i>Effect</i>, or the like; and yet all these and the like, are only <i>Entities of Reason</i> conceived within the mind, that have no more of any real true Existence without it, than Colours have without the Eye, or Sounds without the Ear . . . . It is certain that things to us Men are nothing but as they do stand in our <i>Analogy</i>, that is, in plain terms, <g>they are nothing to us but as they are known by us</g> . . .
